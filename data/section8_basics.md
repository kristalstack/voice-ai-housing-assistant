# Section 8 Basics

Section 8 commonly refers to the Housing Choice Voucher Program, a federal rental assistance program funded by the U.S. Department of Housing and Urban Development (HUD).

The program helps eligible low-income households, older adults, and people with disabilities afford rental housing.

Housing Choice Vouchers are generally administered by local public housing agencies (PHAs).

A household with a voucher usually pays part of the rent based on income. The housing agency pays the remaining approved amount directly to the landlord.

Eligibility depends on factors such as household income, family size, citizenship or eligible immigration status, and local program rules.

There is no single nationwide Section 8 application. Applicants usually apply through a local public housing agency.

Waiting lists may be open, closed, or use lotteries. Availability varies by location.

Applicants may be asked to provide documents such as identification, Social Security numbers if applicable, proof of income, asset information, and household composition information.

Program rules and procedures can vary by housing authority and location.

Housing assistance applications are generally free. Applicants should be cautious of anyone charging fees or guaranteeing voucher placement.
